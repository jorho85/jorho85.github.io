MAT 171 / Precalculus Algebra

Graphing Polynomials and Rational Functions

Use factors to predict where graphs cross, turn, or have a missing point.

Use the arrow keys to advance. Reveal answers as you go.

Plan for about 50 minutes. Use the warm-up to reactivate factoring, then spend roughly 12 minutes on polynomials, 10 minutes on the cancellation discussion, 15 minutes on asymptotes and the worked rational, and the remaining time on the exit problem. The slide about repeated factors and missing range values can be used as an extension.

01 / Warm-up

Factor first, then find the intercepts

\(f(x)=x^2+3x+2\)

  1. Factor \(f(x)\).
  2. Find both \(x\)-intercepts.
  3. Find the \(y\)-intercept.

\(f(x)=(x+1)(x+2)\)

The \(x\)-intercepts are \((-1,0)\) and \((-2,0)\).

The \(y\)-intercept is \((0,2)\).

Keep this factored form. We’ll use it again when we put a factor in the denominator.

Give students one minute individually. Ask why setting a product equal to zero lets us solve two simpler equations. Ask what changes if we set x equal to zero instead.

02 / Graph vocabulary

Six features to read from a graph

\(x\)-intercepts
Where does the graph meet the \(x\)-axis? Set \(y=0\).
\(y\)-intercept
Where does it meet the \(y\)-axis? Set \(x=0\), if allowed.
Domain
Which \(x\)-values are possible inputs?
Range
Which \(y\)-values actually occur?
Increasing and decreasing
As you move left to right, where does \(y\) rise or fall? Give \(x\)-intervals.
End behavior
What does \(y\) do as \(x\to-\infty\) and as \(x\to\infty\)?

A quick graph check. Sweep a vertical line for domain and a horizontal line for range.

Use your hand to sweep across the screen in each direction. Throughout the lesson, list increase and decrease on separate open intervals split at turns and excluded inputs.

03 / Polynomials

The leading term controls the ends

Every polynomial has domain \(\mathbb R\) and a smooth, continuous graph. For a nonconstant polynomial, use its highest-degree term to predict the tails.

DegreeLeading coefficientLeft tailRight tail
EvenPositive\(\uparrow\)\(\uparrow\)
EvenNegative\(\downarrow\)\(\downarrow\)
OddPositive\(\downarrow\)\(\uparrow\)
OddNegative\(\uparrow\)\(\downarrow\)

Try \(g(x)=-2x^5+7x^2-1\). Left up and right down.

Ask students to trace the tails of x squared and x cubed in the air, then reflect each one. An odd-degree polynomial has all real outputs by continuity. An even-degree polynomial has a global minimum or maximum, depending on the sign of the leading coefficient.

04 / Polynomials

Where does the graph cross or bounce?

\(p(x)=x^3-3x+2=(x+2)(x-1)^2\)

Odd multiplicity makes the graph cross the axis.

Even multiplicity makes it touch the axis and turn back.

\((-2,0)\) crosses. The factor occurs once.

\((1,0)\) bounces. The factor occurs twice.

The \(y\)-intercept is \((0,2)\).

Start with the factored form so students can focus on graphing. If asked how to factor the cubic, show x³−3x+2=(x³−x)−2(x−1)=x(x−1)(x+1)−2(x−1), then factor the remaining quadratic. Ask for a sign check on both sides of each zero.

The turning points split the rise and fall intervals. The opposite tails give every real output.

Read turning point x-values from the labeled graph. Students can use a graphing tool to locate turning points in other examples. The zeros give intercepts, but a zero is only an interval boundary when the graph switches direction there. Point out that the graph crosses at −2 while continuing to increase.

06 / Predict, then check

Both tails rise. How low can it go?

\(q(x)=x^4-2x^2+1\)

Factor. Predict intercepts and tails. Use the graph to read the range and the intervals of increase and decrease.

\(q(x)=(x-1)^2(x+1)^2\)

The intercepts are \((-1,0),(1,0),(0,1)\).

Domain \(\mathbb R\). Range \([0,\infty)\).

Increasing on \((-1,0)\) and \((1,\infty)\).

Decreasing on \((-\infty,-1)\) and \((0,1)\).

Allow two minutes for pairs to see a perfect-square trinomial in x². Reveal the graph before the written answers, then ask why zero is included in the range.

07 / Rational functions

Start with the original denominator

A rational function is a quotient of polynomials. Its domain excludes every zero of the original denominator.

Before simplifying

Factor the denominator and write down each excluded input.

After simplifying

Keep those exclusions. Use the reduced formula to predict what happens near each one.

At an excluded input, the graph can approach a finite height or grow without bound in magnitude.

Ask whether an excluded input by itself tells us the shape of the graph. Use the next slide to test their prediction.

08 / Think, then discuss

Does the cancellation work at every input?

\(A(x)=\dfrac{x^2+3x+2}{x+2}=\dfrac{(x+1)(x+2)}{x+2}\)

  1. Which factor divides out?
  2. What does \(A(-2)\) give in the original fraction? What does \(x+1\) give there?
  3. Can you divide out \(x+1\) here? What happens at \(x=-1\)?

\(A(x)=x+1\) for \(x\ne-2\).

\(A(-2)\) is undefined. The line gives \(-1\) there.

\(x+1\) remains in the numerator. \(A(-1)=0\), so \((-1,0)\) is an intercept.

Give pairs time to answer before advancing. Ask what (x+2)/(x+2) equals when x = −2. Students should say undefined, since the fraction is 0/0 there. Then ask why (x+1) survives and what denominator would actually allow that factor to divide out.

09 / Change the denominator

Which factor makes which hole?

For \(A\), \(-1\) is an allowed input and a zero. For \(B\), \(-1\) is an excluded input.

Cover the right panel initially. Ask students to predict what happens if the denominator is x+1 instead, then show it. Have them explain why the graphs use different lines and why each open circle belongs at the height of its own reduced formula.

10 / Read the graph with a hole

A missing input can cost a range value

Formula and domain
\(A(x)=x+1,\ x\ne-2\). The domain is \(\mathbb R\setminus\{-2\}\).
Range
\(\mathbb R\setminus\{-1\}\). Only \(x=-2\) could give \(y=-1\).
Intercepts
\((-1,0)\) and \((0,1)\)
Increasing
\((-\infty,-2)\) and \((-2,\infty)\). No decreasing intervals.
End behavior
\(A(x)\to-\infty\) on the left and \(A(x)\to\infty\) on the right.
Clarify that the line increases across any pair of allowed inputs. The list reports separate continuous intervals, so it splits at the hole. For another example, remove x=1 from y=x². The output y=1 stays in the range because x=−1 still gives it. Ask students to predict whether the missing output can be recovered before giving that example.

11 / Near an excluded input

Hole or vertical asymptote?

If the reduced formula accepts the excluded input, it gives the height of the hole.

If the reduced denominator is still zero there, a vertical asymptote occurs. The graph grows without bound in magnitude on at least one side.

\(g(x)=\dfrac{1}{x+2}\)

\(g(x)\to-\infty\) as \(x\to-2^-\)
\(g(x)\to\infty\) as \(x\to-2^+\)

Have students plug in −2.1 and −1.9 to justify which side goes up. Both the punctured line and this reciprocal exclude −2, but their graphs behave differently as x approaches that value. If a denominator has a squared factor remaining, both sides can approach the same infinity.

12 / Far left and far right

Horizontal asymptotes describe end behavior

If the outputs approach a number \(L\) as \(x\) heads far left or far right, the line \(y=L\) is a horizontal asymptote.

Degrees after simplificationHorizontal asymptoteExample
Numerator degree is smaller\(y=0\)\(\frac{1}{x+2}\)
Degrees are equal\(y=\dfrac{\text{leading coefficient on top}}{\text{leading coefficient on bottom}}\)\(\frac{2x+1}{x-3}\ \Rightarrow\ y=2\)
Numerator degree is largerNo horizontal asymptote\(\frac{x^2}{x-1}\)

A graph can cross a horizontal asymptote. For \(h(x)=1+\frac{x}{x^2+1}\), both tails approach \(1\), and \(h(0)=1\).

For the equal-degree example, divide top and bottom by x to see (2+1/x)/(1−3/x) approach 2. For the crossing example, the added term has the sign of x, so the graph crosses y=1 at x=0. Slant asymptotes can be a later lesson. If asked about a constant reduced function, its graph has that constant height on its domain and its end limit is that same constant.

13 / Worked rational example

Factor, record exclusions, and classify them

\(R(x)=\dfrac{x^2+x-6}{x^2-4}\)

Predict the graph

  1. Factor the top and bottom.
  2. Record the excluded \(x\)-values.
  3. Find a hole or vertical asymptote for each excluded value.

\(\dfrac{(x+3)(x-2)}{(x+2)(x-2)}=\dfrac{x+3}{x+2},\quad x\ne\pm2\)

Hole at \(\left(2,\frac54\right)\)

Vertical asymptote at \(x=-2\)

Ask students which familiar factoring strategy they use for each polynomial. Give them time to work before revealing. Emphasize the difference between 2, which disappears from the reduced denominator, and −2, which remains.

For the x-intercept, solve the reduced numerator equal to zero and check the original domain. For the y-intercept, substitute zero. Before drawing the curves, have students predict the sign of 1/(x+2) and which branch goes down near −2. The open circle and dashed lines are explicit here because calculators can hide holes or appear to join branches.

15 / Worked rational example

Check every branch and every missing output

Increasing
None
Decreasing
\((-\infty,-2)\), \((-2,2)\), and \((2,\infty)\)
Why is \(y=1\) missing?
\(\frac1{x+2}\) can never equal zero.
Why is \(y=\frac54\) missing?
\(1+\frac1{x+2}=\frac54\) gives only \(x=2\), an excluded input.
Range
\(\mathbb R\setminus\left\{1,\frac54\right\}\)
To prove the range is complete, solve y=1+1/(x+2) for x. For y≠1 this gives x=1/(y−1)−2. This x can never be −2 and equals 2 only when y=5/4. Thus all other outputs occur. Have students compare this reasoning with the horizontal sweep from slide 3.

16 / Optional extension

Two exceptions worth checking

One copy of the factor remains

\(\dfrac{x+1}{(x+1)^2}\)

At \(x=-1\), is there a hole or a vertical asymptote?

Vertical asymptote. The reduced denominator is still \(x+1\).

Another input gives the same output

\(\dfrac{x^2(x-1)}{x-1}\)

There is a hole at \((1,1)\). Is \(1\) still in the range?

Yes. The original function is defined at \(x=-1\), where it also gives \(1\).

These checks work well for early finishers. Ask how students would draw a hole on an axis if the reduced formula gives zero at an excluded input. For example, (x+1)²/(x+1) has a hole at (−1,0) and no x-intercept.

17 / Exit ticket

Sketch one on your own

\(T(x)=\dfrac{x^2-x-6}{x^2-9}\)

From the formula

  1. Factor and write the domain.
  2. Find the \(x\)- and \(y\)-intercepts, if they exist.
  3. Locate any hole and asymptotes.

From your sketch

  1. Describe the end behavior.
  2. Give the range and the increasing and decreasing intervals.

Explain one feature using a factor in the original formula.

Allow four to five minutes. If students are stuck, suggest writing the reduced expression as 1−1/(x+3). Keep the next slide hidden until students have committed to a sketch.

18 / Exit ticket debrief

\((-2,0)\) and \(\left(0,\frac23\right)\)

Ask students to compare this graph with R. The negative reciprocal term reverses which side of the horizontal asymptote each branch lies on. If students list 5/6 as an excluded input, ask them to identify the coordinates of the hole and which coordinate belongs to each set.

19 / Review

What can you tell before you graph?

Polynomials

Find intercepts and their multiplicities. Use the leading term for the tails. Test the sign between zeros to place the curve. Use turns and tails to read increasing and decreasing intervals and range.

Rational functions

Record domain exclusions before canceling. Use the reduced formula for holes, vertical asymptotes, and end behavior. Check whether any missing height occurs elsewhere.
